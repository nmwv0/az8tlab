## Evidence and Design Inference Are Different

The following summaries retain existing project citations and reported results with their limits. They motivate optional practice and honest feedback; they do not prove the predict-compare-update sequence improves coding skill.

| Existing source | Reported finding or framing | Limit for Predict |
|---|---|---|
| Shen & Tamkin (2026), AI-assisted skill formation study | Interaction patterns were associated with different learning outcomes (n=52, immediate assessment). | Small single study; not a dedicated trial of this skill or evidence of long-term retention. Do not describe Predict as the tested intervention. |
| Barcaui (2025), academic AI/ML learning study | Reported delayed scores of 57.5% versus 68.5% at 45 days (n=120, d=0.68). | Single institution and subject context, not professional programming. Reduced encoding effort is an interpretation, not an isolated neural mechanism. |
| Cheng et al. (2026), "Sycophantic AI", Science 391, eaec8352 | Project reports judgment harms from sycophantic feedback across 11 experiments (n=6,321). | General judgment tasks; programming transfer is untested. The coding taxonomy is an adaptation, not validation of each feedback rule. |
| Perry (2026), "In Defense of Social Friction", Science | Perspective on disagreement and correction. | Commentary, not an additional controlled experiment. Courtesy is not equivalent to sycophancy. |
| Hermans (2021), The Programmer's Brain | Applied cognitive-science framework for programming and cognitive load. | A framework, not experimental proof of fixed harm levels for types of AI assistance or a one-to-one memory-system mapping. |
| Balepur et al. (2024), "A SMART Mnemonic Sounds like 'Glue Tonic'", EMNLP | Mnemonic preferences and learning outcomes can differ (45 students, 2,684 judgments). | Small mnemonic study; judgments are not participants. It does not justify overriding coding opt-outs or accessibility needs. |
| Fung & Oyibo (2024), Applied Sciences 14(23), 11379, DOI: 10.3390/app142311379 | Scoping review of 12 mnemonic serious-game studies, including how mnemonics are generated. | Review, not a coding intervention. Mapping self-generation or SAVE to Predict is an analytical design analogy, not demonstrated causal equivalence. |
| Kang (2016), spaced repetition review/policy synthesis | Reviews benefits of spaced practice and retrieval. | Not new empirical data. This skill does not schedule or measure spaced practice, and optimal coding intervals are not established here. |
| Kilpatrick & Bundgaard-Nielsen (2025), "Say it like you mean it", Cognition 269, 106406 | Attentional Optimization Hypothesis concerning linguistic vividness. | Existing summaries differ in their mechanistic interpretations; do not treat them as demonstrated instructional outcomes. Applying lexical findings to AI explanations is a conceptual extension, not validated here. |
| Cheng et al. (2026), arXiv:2604.03058 | Follow-on exploring verbalized assumptions and sycophancy. | Preprint, not treated as peer-reviewed here. Intent assumptions are a possible explanation, not a universal cause or a validated intent detector. |

## Public-Safe Design Implications

### Additional reviewed sources — not additions to the historical count

- **Hou, Katmanivong, Wang & Ericson, Enhance Code Understanding through Prompted Self-Explanation with Mixed-Up Code Puzzles: Novice Preferences and Outcomes** ([author PDF](https://xinyinghou.org/assets/publications/papers/isls2026.pdf)). Counterbalanced preference study: n=10, eight preferred Select. Randomized classroom comparison: n=110 (54 puzzle+Select, 56 puzzle-only); immediate gain ANCOVA F(1,102)=4.55, p=.035, partial eta squared=.04. The classroom experiment did not compare Select with Fill. Mental-effort p=.992 is not equivalence. Explanations, options, hints and feedback were bundled; practice time is a post-assignment covariate. Manually refined materials do not validate arbitrary LLM grading. No delayed retention or Predict-specific effect was established.
- **Tang, Liang, Hu & Luo (2025), Systems 13(7):555** ([article](https://doi.org/10.3390/systems13070555), [full text](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/systems/systems-13-00555/article_deploy/systems-13-00555.pdf)). Two intact Grade 7 classes at one Chinese school (52 versus 51), six weekly 40-minute Python lessons, same instructor, Spark training/question guide, supervised practice and independent tool-free final assessment. Reported posttest total means 72.67 versus 64.29 (p=.018), practical skills 37.08 versus 27.29 (p<.001), knowledge 35.60 versus 37.00 (p=.265); cognitive interest p=.014, affective interest p=.078, self-efficacy p=.010. One class per condition confounds class with treatment; student-level tests do not resolve clustering and baseline nonsignificance does not establish equivalence. Pre/post tests differ in content/length; multiple outcomes and rank/U reporting inconsistencies limit precision. Appendix A's cognitive-interest items largely concern perceived understanding/knowledge, not direct curiosity. Nine interviews suggest hypotheses, not isolated mechanisms. No delayed independent retention or adult professional transfer was established. This is not Tang, Cunningham & Walther's PNAS Nexus paper.

**Design inference, not a measured effect:** scaffolding expression and keeping related information together may reduce unnecessary explanation effort; reasoning and feedback are secondary design supports. Offer a few inspectable rationales only when wanted. Neither paper measures neural plasticity, representational geometry, consolidation or calibration-score validity. Tang qualifies blanket AI-harm narratives; it does not isolate a prompting template or safeguard as the cause.

- **Scherer, Siddiq & Sánchez-Scherer (2021), Some Evidence on the Cognitive Benefits of Learning to Code**, Frontiers in Psychology 12:559424 ([full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.559424/full), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.559424)). An **opinion article** synthesizing earlier meta-analyses, not a new trial or meta-analysis. It describes promising, domain-dependent associations with reasoning, creative thinking and mathematical skills. Effects depend on instruction, outcomes and design; randomized active-control studies tend to show lower effects. Transfer needs explicit support and is not automatic. Do not count the opinion and underlying syntheses as independent confirmations. No numerical estimate is borrowed here. No direct evidence on generative AI, Predict, universal intelligence, brain health, decline prevention or durable professional outcomes. AI-era relevance is a present-day practical inference.
- **Borghoff, Minas & Schopp (2026), Software 5:27** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/software5020027)). A 38-student survey and exploratory 42-run/two-task assistant study inform a narrow rule: inspect available artifacts, separate facts from assumptions, and retain discovered constraints through suggestions and checks. They do not establish learning benefits or optimal model settings.

**Not intervention evidence:** Doukakis & Vlamos (2018), JMESSP13420489, is a proposal without original results. Alternative representations remain optional, not learning-style prescriptions. Li/Lee/Wu, DOI 10.1016/j.aej.2025.10.026, remains unresolved without available full text and correction notices; its abstract is not integration evidence.

### Learning to code and broader skills

[Scherer, Siddiq & Sánchez-Scherer (2021)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.559424) discuss possible cognitive benefits of coding education, drawing on earlier research. This perspective offers a rationale for valuing coding practice beyond producing software; it does not establish how long benefits persist, how broadly they transfer, or whether Predict produces them. The Mera and Shen findings above address separate questions and are not validations of Predict.

- Offer prediction and comparison when the user wants practice.
- Give accurate, specific feedback, including uncertainty and legitimate alternatives.
- Reduce unnecessary complexity; adjust explanation to the user's needs.
- Avoid stacked questions and allow processing time.
- Honor direct-answer requests immediately. Learning research does not remove user control.

The User Control Comes First rule in `../SKILL.md` applies throughout. Cognitive load categories in `cognitive-load-classification.md` are heuristics; feedback examples in `elephant-quick-reference.md` are examples, not measurements.

## Claims Not Established

No dedicated randomized trial of this skill in AI-assisted coding is established in this material. Long-term professional outcomes and individual differences remain open questions. Findings from educational, mnemonic, lexical, neuroimaging, or general judgment tasks do not establish a causal chain from AI use to reduced cortical plasticity or prove that Predict prevents skill loss.

Do not infer neural changes, memory windows, cognitive state, or ability from typing or conversation. Do not use a numeric retention result from one study as a prediction for an individual user.

Detailed internal mechanism mappings, unrelated developmental or motor-memory interpretations, and medical/pharmacological comparisons are deliberately outside this behavioral brief. Their omission is not a change to the underlying research corpus and does not imply they were independently disproven or verified.
